# Problem framing and data acquisition

## Business context

This project is based on a simulated Kenyan pharmacy retailer operating 12 branches across six counties: Nairobi, Mombasa, Kisumu, Nakuru, Kiambu and Uasin Gishu. The scenario brings together sales, customer, product, promotion, purchasing and inventory information that would normally be held in separate operational records.

The business needs a clearer way to understand sales performance, customer purchasing behaviour, product contribution, branch performance and stock availability. These insights can support operational decisions, while historical sales and demand information can be used to explore short-term product demand forecasting.

All operational records in this project are synthetic. The results describe the simulated retailer only and should not be interpreted as evidence about actual pharmacy businesses or customers in Kenya.

## Problem statement

When sales, customer, purchasing and inventory records are considered separately, it is difficult to build a consistent view of business performance. The project therefore brings these records together to examine how the retailer performs across branches and products, how identified customers purchase over time, and where inventory availability may affect recorded sales.

The work also prepares a product-branch daily demand history that can later be used to evaluate short-term demand forecasting approaches.

## Project objectives

- Examine sales and profitability over time.
- Compare sales and profit contribution across branches and counties.
- Identify products and categories contributing to sales and profitability.
- Understand repeat purchasing patterns among customers with recorded customer IDs.
- Describe sales patterns associated with promotions without making causal claims.
- Examine inventory movements, stock availability, purchasing and unfulfilled demand.
- Prepare a consistent dataset for short-term product demand forecasting.

## Business questions

1. How do sales and profitability change over time?
2. Which branches and counties contribute most to sales and profit?
3. Which products and categories contribute most to sales, units and gross profit?
4. What do identified customers' purchasing patterns show about repeat business?
5. How do sales and margins compare during promoted and non-promoted periods?
6. Where do inventory records indicate stock availability issues, adjustments or write-offs?
7. How do purchase orders and supplier receipts support branch replenishment?
8. Can short-term product demand be forecast well enough to provide useful replenishment information?

The questions guide the subsequent data preparation, exploratory analysis, SQL work, statistical analysis and predictive modelling.

## Data acquisition and sources

The project uses two categories of data.

### Synthetic operational data

Operational records were generated for this project using `script/generate_synthetic_data.py`. The scenario covers 1 January to 31 December 2025 and includes:

| Dataset | Records |
|---|---:|
| Stores | 12 |
| Products | 150 |
| Customers | 8,000 |
| Transactions | 100,000 |
| Transaction items | 247,794 |
| Sales returns | 1,030 |
| Sales return items | 1,271 |
| Promotions | 60 |
| Inventory movements | 270,156 |
| Suppliers | 24 |
| Purchase orders | 6,593 |
| Purchase order items | 20,584 |
| Opening inventory | 1,800 |
| Price changes | 30 |
| Lost sales log | 2,002 |

Approximately 80% of transaction headers were designed to have a customer ID. The remaining records represent purchases where the customer was not identified. The generator uses a fixed random seed (`20250101`) to support reproducibility in a compatible Python, NumPy and pandas environment.

The product names are generic medicine and healthcare-supply labels. Their catalogue provenance has not yet been tied to a specific external catalogue; prices, costs, customers, suppliers, transactions and all other operational attributes are simulated.

### Public contextual data

Two public contextual datasets were compiled separately from the synthetic operational data:

- **Kenya monthly CPI and annual inflation:** compiled from Kenya National Bureau of Statistics (KNBS) Consumer Price Indices and Inflation Rates publications. Source portal: [KNBS CPI and inflation rates](https://www.knbs.or.ke/cpi-and-inflation-rates/).
- **Kenya public holidays for 2025:** compiled from the [Public Holidays Act (Cap. 110)](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/1912/21/eng%402022-12-31/source) and relevant Kenya Gazette notices.

The CPI data provides national economic context; it is not pharmacy-specific inflation. Public contextual data is stored separately in `data/Public_data/` and is not generated by the synthetic-data script.

## Scope and limitations established at the framing stage

- The business scenario and operational records are simulated.
- Customer-level analysis can only include transactions with an identified customer ID.
- Product-name provenance requires a specific catalogue reference if the names are to be presented as externally sourced.
- Public CPI is contextual information and should not be treated as pharmacy-specific price data.
- The project is an analytical portfolio exercise, not an evaluation of a real pharmacy retailer.
